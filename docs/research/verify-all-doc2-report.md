## How far each part can be trusted

- **Identity, counts and security (very reliable).** §2 is 12/12 and §10 is 10/10. These match the binary exactly:
  - 13,734 instructions, 57 functions, and 18 syscall sites, broken down by type.
  - All 14 per-function instruction counts, the 10/8/92 stats for 0x19780, 133 shifts in dlmm, and 67 solver back-edges.
  - Hardcoded keys: only the operator, Token-2022 and ATA. No DEX program ID is hardcoded.
  - All six Anchor discriminators.
  - OtterSec reports the build as not verified.
  - Upgrade authority, fee payer and the gate key are the same wallet.
  - No admin or withdraw path, a stateless executor, one entry path.
- **Bytecode meaning (weakest part).** Of 135 factual bytecode claims, 91 are confirmed, 32 partly and 12 wrong. The counts are right. What the code *does* is often misread.
- **On-chain behaviour (accurate numbers, weak framing).** Of 45 claims, 34 are confirmed, 10 partly and none wrong. Both of the doc's windows were found in public data:
  - The 1,000-tx window (2026-10-03 23:00:27–23:59:54 UTC) reproduces every §8 share exactly: Custom(3) 754, OK 149, Custom(14) 78, compute failures 13, DEX errors 6.
  - The 400-tx window (about 00:27–01:01 UTC) reproduces 15.75% OK, Custom(3) at 318 of 337 reverts, a median win of +0.0007 SOL and a best win of +0.44 SOL (tx 2S6Y…).
- **Interpretations.** Of 48, 36 are supported, 2 partly, 5 wrong and 5 unsupported. The big picture holds: a private, high-frequency cross-DEX arbitrage bot that loses almost nothing on failed attempts and pays heavily only to win, with the guard deciding the outcome. The doc's labels for what individual functions do, and its explanations of why outcomes happen, do not hold.

## Most consequential errors and the correct facts

1. **The profit threshold is not "net ≥ 0"** (D2-015, 049, 137, 140, 141, 147; mostly wrong or partly). The guard compares net gain against `min_profit`, a per-transaction value in instruction bytes d[8:16], and fails with Custom(3). A second check requires `min_profit + fee_in_quote` (d[0:8]) and fails with Custom(2). The doc's `jsle r1,0` / `jsge r7,0` are high-word sign tests inside a 128-bit comparison. The bot sets `min_profit` at or above its own fee+tip, and 0 of 318 sampled wins lost money after costs. So "a landed trade can still lose after a large tip" is essentially false in practice.
2. **The CPIs are not PDA-signed** (D2-014 wrong, D2-047 partly). All four `sol_invoke_signed_c` sites pass zero signer seeds. They are plain invokes, authorized by 9EwQ's transaction signature.
3. **"Computes the optimal size on-chain" describes a path that never ran** (D2-011, 043, 122, 123, 125, 129, 270).
   - The planner, solver, Clock read and logs run only when bit 0x40 of d[17] is set and the route has exactly 2 hops. That bit was clear in all 936 observed transactions, so the bot sizes trades off-chain.
   - There is no fixed 1e6 "probe": 0xf4240 is the minimum trade size. The two candidates are the adaptive size and the cap.
   - The Clock feeds a copy of Meteora DLMM's `update_references`. It is not a deadline.
4. **Custom(14) is a profit guard, not an overflow check** (D2-191, D2-252). It is the guard on cross-quote (wSOL↔USDC, flag 0x20) routes. Overflow returns Custom(16), and Custom(15) is a spend-cap check.
5. **The XOR-masked "ZeroFi swap_v4" is actually HumidiFi** (D2-172 wrong; 173–176 partly; 178 and 268 unsupported). The amount is XORed with the fixed key 0xc3eabae3ff2eff3b, so no outside spec is needed. ZeroFi's calldata is plaintext (17 B: 0x10 + u64).
6. **The "0x0200 Obric-class" venue is Phoenix** (D2-171). The bytes are a Swap tag followed by an ImmediateOrCancel order packet. Amounts are converted to lots from the market header.
7. **Venue table gaps.** The 42-B swap_v2 form is Orca Whirlpool, not Raydium CLMM (D2-157). About 15% of hops use raw bytes supplied by the bot rather than serializer 0x488, covering Manifest, Tessera-V and ZeroFi (D2-046). PumpSwap×DLMM is the largest pairing (36% by count, about 82% of SOL profit) but not a majority. PumpSwap×Manifest is close behind at 26% and the doc never mentions it (D2-204).
8. **Function roles are mislabelled** (D2-061, 079, 081, 092, 093, 114–116):
   - 0x15e8 (`ata_setup.rs`) runs after the swaps: it harvests Token-2022 withheld fees, then closes empty accounts. ATA creation is done by 0xa00.
   - 0xf0a8 and 0x109a8 are heavy-arithmetic swap-quote simulators, not "no-arithmetic deserializers".
   - The binary contains no CLMM/tick math anywhere.
   - 0x19780 is a u128 divide. Multiplication goes through a separate helper, 0x1b2b8 (139 call sites).
9. **The hop-record schema is wrong** (D2-106). There is no "tag ≥ 0x41" check. The 0x41 test is a range check on byte +1, the hop's account count, which must be 1–64.
10. **Error catalog** (D2-040, 246, 249–251, 254):
    - Code 12 means an account is too *short* (< 245 B), or a zero balance or out-of-range field. It never means "too large".
    - "7" is MissingRequiredSignature and never shows as Custom(7). A wrong signer fails as Custom(5).
    - The unpack-path "3" is InvalidAccountData, not Custom(3).
11. **Economics are presented selectively** (D2-206, 207, 213, 230).
    - A typical win nets about +0.001 to +0.0015 SOL; the median is +0.00146. Only about a third of wins fall in the doc's "+0.002–0.07" range.
    - The "+0.30 SOL" win was a USDC-to-SOL route whose real gain was about +0.001.
    - Tips reach 0.66 SOL, not ~0.2. About 30% of wins pay no priority fee at all.
12. **Smaller bytecode slips:**
    - The entrypoint is pinocchio v0.8's deserializer, not solana-program's (the 128-account cap is right) (D2-036).
    - dlmm has 85 back-edges, not 92 (D2-113).
    - The solver scans liquidity segments using a Newton integer square root. It is not a bisection (D2-117).

## What could not be checked, and why

- **D2-005 (used platform-tools v1.54).** The v1.54 / LLVM 20.1.7 pairing is correct, and v1.57 reproduces every address. Which build the author actually ran cannot be observed.
- **D2-224 (a 20-won / 20-lost deep sample).** No copy of the sample exists. The figures it reports are consistent with real chain values; for example, the 1.295 SOL fee is tx 2w4Uyg…, and reverts cost the base fee.
- **Things landed-only data cannot show** (D2-021, 234, 235). Public RPC returns only transactions that landed, so the true send-to-land rate and "confident shots land more" cannot be tested. The fee asymmetry is better explained by submission channels: tipped transactions that fail never land, so failures only ever pay the base fee.

## Patterns in the errors

- **One root misreading spreads.** Taking a 128-bit sign test as the final comparison produced the "net ≥ 0" error in the TL;DR, §3, §6, §8 and §9, and also the overflow label on Custom(14).
- **Correct counts, wrong meaning.** Panic metadata and instruction counts were read accurately, but the purpose of each function was guessed from its name (ata_setup, v3 = CLMM, mul_div).
- **Unexecuted paths described as normal behaviour.** On-chain sizing, the Clock read and the logs are all gated behind a flag that was never set.
- **Venue guesses from payload shape alone.** ZeroFi/HumidiFi, Obric/Phoenix and Raydium/Orca were all identified without checking the program IDs in actual CPIs.
- **Landed-only metrics.** "Land rate" (14.9% / 15.8%) is really the success share among landed transactions. The numbers are exact on that convention, so they were scored confirmed or partly, not wrong.
- **Window and convention mismatches**, mostly resolved in the doc's favour:
  - Figures are exact in the doc's own windows but vary elsewhere (OK 10–19%, Custom(3) at 86–96% of reverts).
  - "~111 KB" is the .text size; the ELF is 117,792 B and the program-data account 145,197 B.
  - The 24/41/42-B payloads are the serializer's output before the bot appends a suffix.
  - "0.00001 SOL" is the 0.000005 SOL base fee rounded.
- **Single-metric artefacts.** The native+wSOL metric misreads routes that cross SOL and USDC (the +0.30 and -3.44 SOL readings).
